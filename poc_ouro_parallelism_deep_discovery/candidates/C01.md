# C01: Ready physical-operator workers

## 1. Identity

Revision 1. Search family: A. Initial category: recurrence hybrid.

## 2. Mechanism

Group already-ready tasks by immutable physical weight identity instead of logical recurrent depth.

## 3. Relevant model property

The audited Base checkpoints share physical weights across four passes but retain depth-indexed state.
See `reports/ouro_semantics_audit.md` for pinned source locations.

## 4. Target scope

Both Base sizes, fixed R4, and the unchanged adaptive gate are scope targets, not demonstrated gains.
Primary C is 1/2/4/8. Contexts are 1K/4K/8K. P2/P4 require physical tests. P8 remains projected.

## 5. Baseline and saved cost

Compare mode-matched native 1GPU and the strongest correct same-P conventional baseline.
The hypothesized saving is: Possible shared GEMM weight access and reduced physical weight replicas.

## 6. Operation sequence

Request-local complete stack -> published ready tasks -> tied operator workers -> named state owners.

## 7. Legal DAG and ownership

Each task must identify request, token/chunk, recurrent depth, physical layer, layout, and version.
Weights are immutable. Historical KV belongs to its matching committed request/depth/layer prefix.
Current KV remains private until its complete input and commit rule pass.
The generic validator is `scripts/verify_dag.py`. This card is not a completed candidate-specific executable DAG.

## 8. Source of concurrency

Different requests or causally ready prompt chunks. Same-token recurrent states are not independent.

## 9. Exactness and quality

Numerically reordered exact graph.
Quality remains NOT_RUN for a new mechanism.
Use the frozen free-running calibration and held-out protocol before a quality-preservation claim.

## 10. State and memory

Charge unique and replicated weights, full required depth KV, current activations, graph buffers, and candidate scratch separately.
For H=2048, full BF16 KV is 786432 bytes/token at L24/R4 and 1572864 at L48/R4.
These are structural capacities, not measured HBM traffic.

## 11. Messages and topology

Only physical 4/5/6/7 are eligible for P2/P4.
Every message needs a producer, consumer, byte count, and completion dependency.
No zero-cost KV movement or unsupported eight-GPU result is permitted.

## 12. Added costs and critical path

Activation routing, queueing, batching delay, cache-owner reads, and fill/drain.
Use a resource-constrained path. Do not add overlapping intervals or replace serial work with a maximum without ready inputs.

## 13. Nearest work

CDB, grouped GEMM, aliased unrolled PP, LoopSpec mixed-depth batching.
The source audits determine overlap. The initial category is not a novelty claim.

## Evidence update 1

The unchanged exact decode DAG has one recurrent chain per request.
Already-ready requests or prefill chunks can supply additional work.
Their existence does not make later recurrent states of one token available.
See [the frontier contract](../reports/exact_frontier_contract.md).

NanoFlow already batches independent operator work and reuses physical weight pointers.
MPK already supplies persistent task workers and device-side control.
These are stronger generic controls than a host-launched eager baseline.
See [the ownership audit](../reports/operator_ownership_nearest_audit.md) and [the persistent audit](../reports/persistent_execution_redteam.md).

The P2/P4 component lane measures an ideal already-ready supply.
It does not execute a complete legal distributed request.
At P2, Base1.4B, one row, stream medians are 0.03707 ms local, 0.02994 ms TP, and 0.06178 ms remote round-trip.
These are warm same-operator measurements.
They are not full-stack weight-residency measurements.

Current status: `GENERIC_ONLY` for ordinary ready-task pooling.
A genuinely new ownership mechanism remains unestablished.
Reopen only with a concrete ready-work pattern and an incremental benefit over aliased generic batching.
